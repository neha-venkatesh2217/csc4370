Tarantulas are among the largest spiders in the world, with body lengths ranging from 5 to 11 cm (2 to 4.5 in) and leg spans of 8 to 30 cm (3 to 12 in) depending on the species. They have two main body parts: the prosoma (cephalothorax) and opisthosoma (abdomen), connected by a pedicel that allows flexibility. Their chelicerae contain venom glands, and many New World species have urticating hairs that can irritate skin or eyes. Coloration varies widely, including brown, blue, green, orange, and yellow.

Tarantulas occur throughout the United States, Mexico, Central and South America, Africa, much of Asia (including southern Japan), and all of Australia. In Europe, they are found in Spain, Portugal, Turkey, southern Italy, and Cyprus. The Indian subcontinent hosts at least 60 species, mainly in the Western Ghats, with additional species in Pakistan, Nepal, and Sri Lanka.

Tarantulas are generally harmless to humans, though bites can be painful. They are popular in the exotic pet trade, and some species are protected under CITES due to overcollection. heir striking appearance and unique behaviors make them subjects of fascination for arachnid enthusiasts.
